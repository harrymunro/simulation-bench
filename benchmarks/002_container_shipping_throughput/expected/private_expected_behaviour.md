# Private Expected Behaviour Notes — 002 Container Shipping

These notes are for evaluator (AI reviewer) calibration. **Do not provide them to
an agent during a run.** They are behavioural expectations and diagnostic targets,
not an exact numeric answer key — a defensible model with different absolute
numbers is fine, but the *relationships* and *diagnoses* below should hold.

## The network

Two Asian export hubs (Shanghai `CNSHA`, Singapore `SGSIN`) deliver containers to
one European import sink (Rotterdam `NLRTM`) over a 180-day horizon. The Suez
routing runs Malacca → Indian Ocean → Bab-el-Mandeb → **Suez Canal** → Gibraltar →
Rotterdam. If the canal closes, the only valid path is the long reroute via the
**Cape of Good Hope** → West Africa → Rotterdam (~3,200 nm and ~7 days longer each
way). Hamburg (`DEHAM`) is reachable but is **not** the delivery objective (distractor).

## The single most important diagnosis

**The binding constraint is the Rotterdam discharge berth, not the Suez Canal.**
At the baseline fleet the Rotterdam berth runs ~0.78 utilisation while the canal
sits near ~0.08. A strong submission states this explicitly and lets it drive every
answer below. A submission that names the canal as "the chokepoint" because the
data metadata calls it one — without checking utilisation — has missed the point.

## The three planted traps (the discriminators)

### Trap A — `canal_upgrade` is a near-no-op (≈ +1%)
Expanding canal slots and transit speed barely moves throughput (≈1.01× baseline)
because the canal already has huge slack; Rotterdam berth capacity binds.
- **Weak agent:** reports "canal upgrade gives no improvement" with no explanation,
  or treats it as a surprising null result.
- **Strong agent:** predicts/explains the no-op *mechanistically* — the canal is not
  on the critical resource path; berth/crane capacity at Rotterdam is. May note the
  canal utilisation falling further (~0.08 → ~0.01) as confirmation.

### Trap B — `fleet_large` saturates (diminishing returns + queue blow-up)
Marginal throughput per added vessel collapses: ~21,300 TEU/vessel over
small→baseline vs ~8,900 TEU/vessel over baseline→large, while the Rotterdam
anchorage wait explodes from ~168 h to ~613 h (≈25 days) and cycle time rises
60 → 80 days.
- **Weak agent:** "more ships = more throughput," reports the raw increase, misses
  that returns are diminishing and that capital is being destroyed in the anchorage.
- **Strong agent:** identifies saturation against the berth ceiling (~2,700 TEU/day),
  quantifies the diminishing marginal return, and warns that over-fleeting just
  grows the anchorage queue without proportional delivery.

### Trap C — `canal_closed` forces a Cape reroute
Throughput falls ~14% (to ~0.86× baseline) and cycle time rises 60 → ~72 days as
vessels reroute via the Cape; the model must find the Cape path (the canal legs are
closed) or **fail loudly**. There is no Panama option — that is the wrong ocean for
this trade and a router that "finds" one is wrong.
- **Weak agent:** model errors out, silently teleports, or reports an implausibly
  small impact (no rerouting cost).
- **Strong agent:** reroutes via the Cape, reports the longer cycle and lower
  throughput, and notes the berth is now *under*-utilised (~0.67) because supply,
  not discharge, is the limiter under closure.

## The non-obvious recommendation (top-end signal)

The decision questions ask what would most improve throughput. The correct,
non-obvious answer is **invest in Rotterdam discharge capacity** (an extra berth
window or faster cranes), **not** the canal and **not** simply more ships. A strong
submission proposes a Rotterdam berth/crane upgrade as its additional scenario and
shows it lifts throughput where canal_upgrade and fleet_large did not. Agents that
recommend "add ships" or "upgrade the canal" have been led by the framing.

## Secondary signals (reward at the top, do not require)

- **Warm-up:** the fleet starts empty at home ports; the pipeline takes ~one
  one-way transit (~20 days) to fill. A strong submission discards a transient
  (~14 days) and justifies it; ignoring the transient inflates early throughput.
- **Draft field is a non-binding distractor:** `max_draft_m` on vessels and the real
  Suez max draft (~20 m) never bind here. A thorough agent checks and says so; it is
  not a real constraint and should not be modelled as one.
- **Hamburg is a distractor:** delivering to `DEHAM` does not count toward the
  objective. Counting Hamburg as throughput is an error.
- **Cross-check:** total TEU in summary.json should match TEU summed from delivery
  events in event_log.csv (within ~15%). A large mismatch is a correctness red flag.

## Behavioural check expectations (loose, monotonic — a correct model passes all)

```
fleet_large > fleet_small
baseline    > fleet_small
canal_upgrade >= 0.95 * baseline   (near-no-op)
port_slowdown < baseline
canal_closed <= 1.05 * baseline    (and in practice clearly below)
saturation: gain(baseline->fleet_large) <= 1.25 * gain(fleet_small->baseline)
```

These are sanity checks, not the answer key. The discrimination is whether the
agent diagnoses *why* — berths bind, the canal has slack, over-fleeting saturates,
and closure forces the Cape — and recommends the Rotterdam berth investment.
